Before delivery day

Hot tub delivery and backyard access in Pittsburgh

Western Pennsylvania yards are not always flat, wide or simple. Measuring the route before purchase prevents expensive surprises.

Measure the entire route

Check gates, side yards, deck openings, retaining walls, air-conditioning units, roof overhangs, utility meters and any tight turns. The spa normally travels on its side during parts of a standard delivery.

Think vertically too

Steps, steep grades, elevated decks and major retaining walls can change the equipment needed. Some properties require specialized handling or a crane. Those situations should be identified before the delivery is scheduled.

Prepare the foundation

A filled hot tub is heavy. It needs a stable, level foundation appropriate for the model and site. Concrete is common, but other engineered options may be suitable depending on the spa and manufacturer's requirements.

Leave room for future service

Do not design the backyard so tightly around the cabinet that technicians cannot reach service panels or equipment. A beautiful installation should still be maintainable.

Send photos early

Photos and measurements can help our delivery team identify obvious access concerns before delivery day. For unusual Pittsburgh-area sites, an on-site review may be appropriate.

What to measure before choosing a spa

Measure the clear opening of each gate with the gate fully open, not just the distance between fence posts. Note the narrowest section of the route, sharp turns, changes in grade, steps and the distance from the unloading area to the planned spa location. The clearance needed depends on the spa's actual dimensions and how it must be moved; do not assume a gate will work from the spa's advertised width alone.

Five useful photos for a delivery review

  • The driveway or unloading location.
  • Every gate or narrow passage, with a tape measure visible if possible.
  • Any steps, retaining walls or steep slopes along the route.
  • Overhead obstacles such as low branches, decks or roof overhangs.
  • The proposed spa pad and the space around it for future access.

For yards with soft ground, gravel, steep slopes or limited turning room, tell the team about those conditions in advance. Rain and seasonal ground conditions can also affect the plan.

Questions to settle before delivery day

Confirm the exact model and dimensions, where the spa will sit, whether the base is ready, what delivery services are included and whether special equipment may be required. Coordinate the electrical work separately with a qualified electrician using the spa's model-specific specifications; see our Pennsylvania hot tub electrical planning guide.
